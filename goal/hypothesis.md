# Falsifiable Research Hypotheses & Mechanisms

## Hypotheses

### Hypothesis 1 (Social Insurance & Pension Dominance):
In a mature welfare state with an aging demographic structure (*Urnenform*), social security contributions and federal tax subsidies destined for mandatory social insurances (statutory pensions GRV, statutory health GKV, and long-term care SPV) represent the single largest fiscal per-capita stream ($> 45\%$ of all fiscal flows per capita), exceeding combined public investments in infrastructure, schools, universities, and defense.

### Hypothesis 2 (Multi-Level Institutional Specialization):
Functional public spending exhibits strict institutional separation across federal levels:
- Primary and secondary education (schools, teachers) and tertiary higher education (universities) are concentrated almost entirely at the State (Bavaria) and Municipal (Munich) levels, with $< 5\%$ federal funding share.
- National defense, international diplomacy, and nationwide rail/motorways are concentrated exclusively at the Federal level.
- Early childhood daycare, local urban mobility, and municipal housing are primarily funded at the City level.

### Hypothesis 3 (Munich Fiscal Concentration & Equalization Outflow):
Due to high economic productivity and headquarters concentration, Munich generates exceptionally high per-capita revenues via *Gewerbesteuer* and income tax share ($> 2.5\times$ the German municipal average), but this is substantially redistributed through the Bavarian municipal fiscal equalization (*Kommunaler Finanzausgleich*), the *Bezirksumlage*, and the nationwide *Länderfinanzausgleich* (where Bavaria transfers $> €9\text{ billion}$ annually to other states).

### Hypothesis 4 (Denominator Sensitivity - Zensus 2022 Shock):
Applying the official Zensus 2022 population revisions reduces the resident denominator by $\sim 1.4\text{ million}$ nationwide and $\sim 80,000$ in Munich, which mechanically increases all calculated per-capita fiscal contributions and public expenditures by $+1.5\%$ to $+5.3\%$ without any change in real budgetary outlays.
